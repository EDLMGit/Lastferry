# Wide Berth — translate the visual target into the game

Open `WIDE_BERTH_VISUAL_TARGET.png` at readable size. It was generated for this corrective task using the user's rejected screenshot, the earlier accepted Driftwood frame and the current portrait launch as references. It is concept art, not an actual runtime capture.

## What the board establishes

### A ferry with a recognizable cabin and living passengers

Use a curved yellow hull with enough visible thickness, a red cabin/roof that has a clear silhouette, an open timber deck and a few restrained fenders. The passengers must be the living focal point. The deck reads as a working space with a group aboard, not a vast flat tray with a tiny box at one end.

The main study shows the relevant five-body/six-space arrangement: one sheep and four rabbits. Take its readable separation, relative scale and cabin/deck hierarchy as guidance. Derive real positions and clear transfer routes from actual production geometry. The drawing's rail/rope arrangement is not a collision plan.

Keep the existing sheep and rabbit models/appearance identities; no new detailed animal sculpt, fur renderer or facial rig is required. Achieve readability through proportion, staging, orientation, lighting already available and clear silhouettes. Do not shrink animals to fit or replace them with portrait billboards.

### Shores that feel like different places

Harbor is an asymmetrical protected refuge, Wide Berth a working shoreline, High Quay an angled stone rise, and Shell Landing a low reed/shell spit. The board's four studies communicate different outlines, edge material and land use. They are not a new map topology or exact elevation diagram.

Use restrained existing props and open space around waiting groups. The shape of land must do some of the visual work. Keep useful piers short and compact, with small seams between closely fitted boards; keep the refuge/waiting area on supported land. Rock and timber need distinct construction, not one repeated slatted rectangle in two colors.

The existing renderer's triangle fan needs compatible outlines. An asymmetric teardrop/irregular convex spit is a valid translation of the Shell study if a concave notch would require unnecessary triangulation changes. A genuinely concave authored coast is acceptable only if triangulation, masks and clearance are checked correctly.

### A launch with believable contact

The bottom study anchors shore, supports, quay, boat and water together. Characters' feet visibly belong to the quay, and the hull belongs to the water. The boat and dock are close enough to understand a released mooring line. The same boat silhouette should be recognizable in 2D and 3D.

Preserve the established quiet Canvas illustration vocabulary. Do not copy the board as a static shot or add richer lighting that changes the rest of the story. The actual eight-second scene must show release, glide and settle without Nell appearing on the roof, an unsupported wall or a gap of distant landscape under the quay.

## What not to copy literally

- This board has no authoritative coordinates, depths, capacity labels or UI.
- Incidental shore figures and props are not new characters or requirements. Follow the actual two-sheep/four-rabbit roster; a five-body load leaves the other sheep safely ashore.
- The board's isometric cameras, extra surface detail, stronger reflections, body rounding and shading are illustrative. Preserve the game's accepted lighting/water and mobile performance approach.
- The lower panel is a composition reference, not a new camera direction required at the expense of readable captions or controls.
- Do not redesign menus, add waves/particles, change the palette globally or import new rendering systems to imitate the image.
- Do not paste it behind clickable hotspots or label it as evidence of an implemented fix.
