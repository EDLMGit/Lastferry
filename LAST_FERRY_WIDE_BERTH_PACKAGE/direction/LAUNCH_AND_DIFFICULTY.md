# Direction — A wider deck and the first full-load decision

## The campaign adjustment

The accepted story direction already moves from Nell's rescue to a six-space boat, then larger passenger sizes, combined loading decisions and changing currents. Keep that progression. This milestone changes the strength of C4, not the campaign's identity.

Wide Berth was previously described as a forgiving deep-water introduction. It now demonstrates capacity in a clear opening pickup and continues into a substantial rescue choice. The later current should feel like another layer of planning rather than the first meaningful challenge.

| Stage | Player learns or combines | Difficulty role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Holding / Bellwater | Actions, draft, tides, consequences and Undo | Established introduction |
| Driftwood / Nell | A safe intermediate landing can make a rescue possible | Accepted staging lesson |
| **Wide Berth — current task** | Six spaces, full-load draft four, deep detour versus useful staging, two occupied flood boundaries | Clear capacity benefit followed by consequential planning |
| Orchard Landing / Orin | A three-space neighbor changes feasible combinations | Clear species introduction followed by a real mixed-load puzzle |
| Two Landings | Mixed one/two/three-space loads, route depth and staging | Stronger synthesis |
| Fixed then changing current | Direction first; later forecasted reversal | A new water rule after the existing choices are understood |

Only Wide Berth and its launch are implemented now. Preserve breathing room later in the campaign and the warm final homecoming. “Increasing difficulty” is not a requirement that every new scene be harder than every previous one.

The next encounter should ask the player to compare plans, not read more instructions. Keep time tied to committed actions, allow free thought/inspection and preserve full Undo. The supplied proof shows feasible strategies and consequential alternatives; it does not measure human difficulty. After the user plays, use which choice they understood and which surprised them to judge calibration.

Driftwood requires discovering staging. Wide Berth gives a valid full-delivery route and makes staging an optional way to rescue the second landing earlier. That strategic freedom is the new step; it is not proof of a harder puzzle. Do not delete the full route by simply tightening a flood date to force a repetition of Driftwood. If the user experiences the cove as automatic errands, revisit the decision structure with new proof before treating the difficulty goal as achieved.
