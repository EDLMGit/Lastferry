# Last Ferry — story scenes for The Long Tide

**Status:** authored direction and caption copy, not implemented scenes, tested footage or supplied video. The first task includes only Scene 1 and the short Holding → Bellwater connective beat. The other five major scenes belong to their later content milestones.

The campaign has four phases of three coves. Nell joins in Cove 3; a prepared larger ferry launches afterward. Cove 4 teaches its six spaces before Cove 5 introduces Orin. Cove 11 is the hardest puzzle. Cove 12 is a gentle, short final rescue, followed by supper.

## Shared presentation contract

Use small in-engine dioramas and the established miniature world. **Six to twelve seconds of settling motion** is the target for each major scene, not a reading deadline or minimum wait. Captions remain until the player advances. Keep Skip visible throughout; no caption advances automatically. A settled scene can remain on screen as long as the player wishes.

Opening captions may advance individually through Next; the final caption offers Continue. **Skip animation** settles the scene immediately and reveals all of its caption copy together. Continue alone leaves that readable end frame for the intended gameplay boundary. Settled, skipped, Reduced Motion and restored opening states retain all three opening lines. Every activation advances once. Cancel pending story motion and callbacks on Skip animation, navigation, restart or scene disposal.

Story presentation spends no turns and changes no tide, passenger, capacity or delivery state. During a scene, suspend puzzle input and animation-driven action commitments. Decorative scene arrangements must remain separate from canonical puzzle state; never create a scene by moving real delivered passengers back onto a low pier. Load the next authored puzzle only through the campaign transition.

Respect existing Sound/Motion settings and audio activation intent. Use the established score and audio context; never create an independently unmuted story context. Do not make an essential cue depend on sound. Captions convey the complete story when muted. Reduced motion uses the composed endpoint or static cuts, preserves all copy and controls, and never forces a camera sweep. No pre-rendered video, voice acting or lip-sync is required.

At phone sizes, protect the ferry, relevant character silhouette and caption/control safe areas. Let portrait and landscape use different authored framing. Avoid letterbox bars, tiny cinematic subtitles, distant characters and covered buttons. The caption may wrap and grow; the composition must accommodate it. At very short heights, a stable view is preferable to a zoom. Touch, natural keyboard focus and visible Skip/Continue remain usable.

All Home harbors are protected piers around the same lighthouse island, seen through different coastal vignettes. Delivered characters stay safe. Returning appearances are at the refuge. Cosmetic dusk advances through chapter milestones; neither music nor scene duration changes the local puzzle forecast.

## 1. The first evening

**Trigger:** starting the story, before Holding Pier. **First-task scope.**

**Composition:** begin close enough to recognize the yellow hull and red cabin beside the familiar shoreline. Show the lighthouse against luminous water, then settle into the accepted playable Holding framing. Keep the captain unseen. The boat and any animals shown must agree with the real starting arrangement; scenery-only camera views can avoid implying an invented departure.

**Caption sequence, exact:**

1. “Your first evening alone at the helm.”
2. “The tide will be high tonight.”
3. “Bring everyone to the lighthouse.”

Each line waits for the player. The final Continue reveals the untouched initial puzzle. The opening should feel like an invitation to care for this place. No ticking clock, flashing threat or approaching storm wall is needed.

**Assets:** existing ferry, water, coastline, lighthouse, painted reading material, controls and music. Add the smallest reusable story-presentation family needed; register actual new assets when implemented. No new character, boat or environment asset is required for this scene.

## Connective beat — Holding to Bellwater

**Trigger:** the player chooses Continue from Holding's completed rescue. **First-task scope.** This is a short connective beat, not a seventh major cinematic.

**Composition:** keep the safe Harbor arrival visible, with Pip, Rue and Moss already home. An existing warm lamp and calm water are sufficient. A subtle change in framing may hint at the next part of the coast, but new scenery and travel animation are unnecessary.

**Caption, exact:** “Pip, Rue and Moss are safe. Across the water, Bellwater is still waiting.”

Skip animation settles this readable frame; Continue enters Bellwater through the campaign boundary. The beat must not re-board the rescued trio, advance either puzzle's tide or imply they are Bellwater's passengers. Tansy, Lark and Bram remain Bellwater's own residents.

**Assets:** the completed Holding scene and shared story presentation. Use existing music behavior; do not replay victory audio solely because this beat opens.

## 2. A wider deck

**Trigger:** after completing Cove 3, Driftwood Yard, when continuing to Phase II. **Future milestone.**

**Composition:** Nell is safe at the protected boathouse. Her larger spare ferry is already built and ready beside it. A mooring releases; the wider yellow hull settles onto the water. Pip, Rue and Moss watch from the high refuge pier. Finish with a readable view of the larger passenger deck and familiar red cabin.

**Caption:** “Nell had a bigger boat ready. Now there are hands to launch it.”

This is a launch, not a rapid construction montage during an emergency. Cove 4 subsequently introduces six spaces through the actual controls and preview. No upgrade shop, currency or hull picker appears in the story scene.

**Assets:** Nell's approved beaver model/portrait, six-space ferry, a protected boathouse vignette and reusable rope/mooring prop. Only implement these with their corresponding content milestone and inventory entries.

## 3. The orchard neighbor

**Trigger:** entering Cove 5, after the larger boat's introductory cove. **Future milestone.**

**Composition:** a few orchard leaves frame a landing. Orin steps into a clear view, his short antlers and larger silhouette readable on a phone. Settle into the playable cove while he waits under its authored starting forecast.

**Caption:** “Orin knows every path through the orchard. Tonight, he needs the ferry.”

The real passenger choice teaches Orin's three-space cost. Do not suggest the old four-space ferry could not carry him alone, or that a cinematic creates his cost. His identity needs no hidden ability or special emotional rule.

**Assets:** Orin's approved deer model/portrait and a restrained orchard environment family. The small orchard bough may later recur at supper as decoration, without becoming cargo or inventory.

## 4. The water turns

**Trigger:** after completing Cove 6, entering Phase III. **Future milestone.**

**Composition:** Lark watches from a safe high pier. Broad floating reeds reveal an established current flowing through the channel beside a clear marker. Open the view toward the next cove, keeping the water luminous and slow. Do not depict a reversal here: Cove 7 teaches a fixed direction, and Cove 8 introduces its first forecasted change.

**Caption:** “Beyond the headland, the tide chooses another way.”

The following cove explicitly teaches the actual forecasted current rule. This poetic line cannot substitute for direction and next-tide information. The scene itself never changes route legality, pushes the ferry or consumes a turn.

**Assets:** approved current presentation, its readable marker and modest reused reeds. Their movement follows the same authored meaning as the subsequent rule; reduced motion retains a static directional indication and text.

## 5. The last lights

**Trigger:** after completing Cove 9, entering Phase IV. **Future milestone.**

**Composition:** more refuge windows glow warmly. Tansy unfolds a cloth, Moss settles beside the table and Bram stands near the small bell. Across the water, a remaining occupied landing holds a light. Keep the scene composed around a few representatives rather than a dense crowd.

**Caption:** “The table is nearly ready. A few lights are still waiting across the water.”

The next phase offers a quiet crossing, the hardest synthesis at Cove 11, and a short final rescue at Cove 12. The lighting gives the journey direction without implying a real-time countdown.

**Assets:** a small supper setting, warmer refuge-window arrangement and approved recurring props. Reuse existing characters. The bell is optional sound; the scene reads completely while muted.

## 6. A place for the captain

**Trigger:** after the final visible delivery in Cove 12 and explicit continuation from its arrival. **Future milestone.**

**Composition:** the last neighbors join the safe pier. Open the view toward supper: Bellwater's cloth, Nell's rope by the mooring and Orin's orchard bough connect earlier encounters. One place remains empty, facing the arriving ferry. The lighthouse's accepted warm sweep crosses the gathering once, then the composition rests.

**Caption, exact:** “We saved you a place.”

Let the player linger with the water and quiet score. Continue leads to credits with clear Replay coves and Settings access. There is no death revelation, hidden sacrifice, new quest marker or unresolved cliffhanger. The final crossing is a gentle release after Cove 11's challenge.

**Assets:** the approved refuge/supper setting and a small representative cast. Keep the captain unseen. Use the existing audio intent and score language; any later musical ending must receive its own listening review.
