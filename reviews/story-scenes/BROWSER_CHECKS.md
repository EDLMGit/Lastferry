# Story scenes — focused browser verification

Seven distinct focused cases pass across the retained runs. The original five-case batch is **four passed / one failed**, not relabeled as a clean run. The failure was Chromium rejecting the harness's attempt to resize an operating-system window while it was fullscreen. The corrected lifecycle case and the additional Harbor mode variants pass in a targeted two-case follow-up. No application change or relaxed viewport assertion was required for that correction.

## Environment and commands

Windows, Node 24.15.0, npm 11.12.1, Playwright 1.64.1, Chromium 156.0.8078.4, SwiftShader, DPR 1. Actual HTML controls were driven with trusted Chromium emulated touch, mouse and natural Tab/Shift+Tab/Enter. These are native browser API checks at native phone dimensions, not physical Android captures.

## Capture method and source identity

The test's observer reads timeline, actual actor transforms, camera projection, caption text fragments and native control bounds in one in-page RAF sequence. It does not change time, transform an animal, inject a puzzle state or pause the world. This avoids the original screenshot-RPC timing miss, preserved in [development observations](PRELIMINARY_BROWSER_RUNS.md).

`node tools/capture-story-scenes.mjs reviews/story-scenes/browser --media` extracts representative native frames and three short continuous WebP excerpts from the actual browser recordings. The full original recordings remain local/ignored. Recorded wall/timeline samples supply a search window only. Final boundaries are calibrated against actual source pixels: matching the static complete-caption region identifies the scene's first visible frame, and matching the normal gameplay HUD identifies its handoff. The search samples at 0.1-second cadence; each chosen frame records its exact original decoded frame index/PTS and is inspected at native size. This is not a subframe controller-clock claim. Read-only RAF samples independently establish actual runtime acknowledgment timing.

Pip's representative frame is selected by inspecting the native source sequence, not by assuming that rendered caption time equals controller time. A contact sheet revealed that the earlier fixed one-second selection was already descending. The complete unscaled 844×390 frame at source **25.68 seconds** shows the lifted-foot gap and turn near the visible hop peak; its selection is keyed to the original recording's SHA in [gesture-selections.json](browser/gesture-selections.json). The enlarged diagnostic contact sheet stays local/raw and is not presented as native gameplay evidence. Last-scene-frame selections precede the content-matched normal HUD; the desktop last-before-handoff frame still shows Skip and is not claimed to be a watched settled frame.

The three WebP excerpts retain native dimensions and natural elapsed time with no internal cuts, camera cropping, interpolated frames or speed change. The declared trim uses source content, removing title loading and later unrelated play while preserving the visible finite scene and gameplay handoff. Both source frames and the final frames decoded from all three derivatives are inspected to confirm the actual normal Holding/Bellwater HUD. Footage is silent visual evidence; source/output hashes, matching scores, real frame PTS, sample rate, durations and decoder results are in [video-frames.json](browser/video-frames.json).

## Original failure and remaining limits

- The original lifecycle case reached actual native fullscreen and keyboard Skip, then `page.setViewportSize` requested an operating-system window resize while that window was fullscreen. Chromium rejected `Browser.setWindowBounds` with “restore it to normal state first.” [Original context](browser/original-fullscreen-resize-context.md) and the four-pass/one-failure result remain. The corrected test uses real native fullscreen exit before the viewport resize and separately re-enters/exits fullscreen through Settings. It does not mock the API, weaken the target assertions or establish physical Android rotation inside fullscreen.
- The first wall-clock-only video extraction was misaligned: the file named 780px opening beginning actually showed the title, and its estimated clip ends could not establish the gameplay handoff. The [original metadata](development-alignment/original-video-frames.json) and [misnamed title frame](development-alignment/780x320-original-title-as-begin.png) are retained. All six source recordings and all three excerpts were recalibrated by actual caption/HUD pixels and original frame PTS; boundaries and affected native frames were regenerated and re-inspected. This was an evidence-extraction defect, not an application failure. The original excerpts remain locally ignored with their hashes preserved.
- Hide/show uses controlled `document.hidden` and `visibilitychange`, not a physical operating-system background/throttling test. Audio activation ownership is checked; no new human listening approval is claimed.
- Physical Android, other engines, native GPU/performance, alternate platform fonts and screen-reader operation are unverified. The inherited overlap, delivered-heading and rapid-touch limitations remain open. Full mid-puzzle resume remains outside this correction.
- The new scenes are implemented and verified for the stated coverage. Human judgment of warmth, gesture readability and pacing remains separate from browser assertions and source audits.
