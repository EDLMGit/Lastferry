## The planning decision

On the shallow first trip the ferry can take two spaces. It must leave exactly six to bring home together after the channel deepens. Knowing the load arithmetic is not enough: by the second turn of the current, the empty ferry must already be back across Pomona Reach and nobody can still be waiting on flooding Spring Steps.

Both tested strategies finish in 13 actions. One brings Burr and Sloe up from the Steps, leaves Sloe at the orchard and takes Burr home first. The other takes Brindle home first, uses the return window to collect the Steps pair and reunites the final group at the orchard. Choosing only Sloe at the first Steps visit still leaves the game playing but exhausts all winning continuations. The [design and counterfactuals](DESIGN.md) distinguish that first losing choice from the later visible loss, document the exact minimum and justify the developer-only target. The app does not show those conclusions to the player.


## Proof and actual play

[Machine-readable production proof](audit-results.json) · [committed action witnesses](../../data/drowned-orchard/witnesses.json) · [reproducible audit](audit.mjs). The search exhausted 326 canonical states and 880 legal transitions. The first trip must carry exactly two spaces: one later trip can carry at most six, and the opening sill admits at most two. Switching **which** two-space neighbor goes changes when the Steps pair must be handled and where the ferry can be at the reversal. The Sloe-only decision is already irrecoverable on action 2 (46 reachable continuations, no win). The explicit terminal loss comes on action 4, when crossing home leaves seven spaces offshore. Undoing three actions from that loss gives a different winning continuation. The mathematical minimum is **13**; the proposed efficient target is also **13** because both materially different strategies qualify. It has not been registered or accepted.
