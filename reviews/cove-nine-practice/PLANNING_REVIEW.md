## A concrete design direction to commission

Suggested design question:

> With the orchard's lower water now changed, which side of the turning reach must the ferry and the remaining neighbors occupy, and what must be completed before the next change?

Treat this as a starting hypothesis for authoring and exhaustive proof. A useful candidate would make the player reconsider a familiar landing or passage, infer a future load/access obligation, and alter an earlier pickup, delivery or positioning choice to preserve it. A later increase in depth can coincide with losing a useful direction or shore; “wait for more water” must be evaluated through the actual rules rather than assumed helpful.

Require the author to state **what the returning player must infer here that neither accepted Orchard nor Ternhaven already requires**:

| Existing cove | Accepted reasoning to distinguish from |
| --- | --- |
| Orchard Landing | Deliver Pomme first so Medlar can accompany Orin through depth-three water during the only outward window; quay staging and returning to Spring Steps offer two deliberate approaches. |
| Ternhaven | Fulfill the early delivery while choosing a staging location whose later retrieval order and approach remain feasible; High Bank and flooding Tern Steps lead to different plans. |
| Proposed C9 | Reinterpret the familiar place under changed conditions, with a consequential relationship between future position/access and an earlier load or rescue obligation. Exact dependency remains to be authored and proved. |

Do not accept merely renamed Orchard passengers with the same escort partition, or Ternhaven's two-refuge choice moved into orchard scenery. Conversely, do not mandate a second current, extra mechanic, extra species or many more nodes just to force novelty.

Keep recognizable geographic anchors and visual orientation. A compact candidate-local adjustment to route access, usable landing, passenger arrangement or tide/current schedule can be justified in DESIGN; preservation of place does not require copying every numeric C7 value. Do not modify the accepted Orchard level to create the revisit.

**Concrete initial-state risk:** in the accepted Orchard, Cider Quay and Spring Steps have elevation 1. Both flood at tide level 1 under `tideLevel < elevation`. Simply raising initial tide while copying its waiting group produces a materially different, potentially invalid opening. The candidate must explicitly reconcile changed water, dry waiting places, berth/land support and all current phases. Do not rely on visual submersion disconnected from gameplay truth.

Use fresh stable character IDs/names and explicit existing appearance keys. The title and proposed new ID `drowned-orchard` are suitable working choices, not an already implemented registry entry. Four accepted species/five appearances and current individual space costs suffice.

## Proof and acceptance criteria

Reuse COVE_EIGHT_PRACTICE_01's successful evidence structure, adjusted to C9's actual claims:

- Prove the candidate using production `legalActions` and reducer, then replay committed witnesses through the real Session. A separate approximate simulator cannot establish the shipped result.
- Establish the exact minimum by exhaustive breadth-first search or equivalent complete evidence. Enumerate every legal transfer subset. Preserve exact turns through the last authored change; explain any sound plateau-state normalization rather than truncating late recoveries.
- Show the actual early decision, the later constrained state and the dependency joining them. Isolate each claimed current/location and load/timing effect with separate developer-only counterfactuals. If relaxing a proposed constraint changes nothing, weaken the claim or revise the design.
- Exhaust every continuation of at least one **plausible legal mistake** before calling it unwinnable. Identify its first consequential decision and explain the later visible capacity/depth/direction/deadline conflict. Distinguish still-playing unwinnability from an actual terminal failure.
- Require two consequentially different deliberate winning approaches before proposing a target. They should differ in meaningful order, placement, positioning or approach; harmless waits, symmetric identity swaps and scripts merging before the real decision do not count as strategic freedom.
- Test obvious shortcuts: early collection of everyone who fits, immediate Harbor shuttles, safe waiting for the final tide, and every ordinary-route detour available in the candidate. Legitimate alternatives are useful results, not defects to hide.
- Execute the tempting mistake, Undo to the real decision, then complete an alternative rescue with correct locations, tide, identities and non-rewindable successful-Undo history.
- Record the proved minimum and **proposed** efficient target separately, selecting the latter after considering both deliberate approaches. Do not prescribe a number or require it to exceed C8's 13/14 figures.
- If the desired dependency does not survive production proof, revise candidate data or report the failed premise. Do not broaden shared rules, freeze a weak puzzle or present an unproved candidate as complete.

Known rule bounds are sufficient: six spaces; drafts 1/2/3/4 for empty/1–2/3–4/5–6 occupied spaces; one action per committed move, nonempty transfer or Wait; action before tide/loss/win; complete Undo; ordinary rescue objective. `checkCurrents` supports **at most one** current passage and demands an explicit direction for every reachable tide. Other edges are bidirectional. No locks, cargo, inventory, fuel, timer, new boat, independent current clock, extra global failure detector or new reward system.

Facts needed to plan must remain inspectable with Tips Off. Do not place the developer solution over the ordinary map.
